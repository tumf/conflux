## Implementation Notes

- The scope lives in `src/ai_command_runner.rs` as `RunCommandScope`: a clone-shared `Arc` over one `std::sync::Mutex` (admission flag plus registration map), a `CancellationToken` runner tasks observe directly, and a `Notify` the barrier waits on. Final admission and `Command::spawn` share that mutex, which is what makes "closed" and "already spawned" mutually exclusive rather than merely ordered.
- `CommandQueue::execute_with_stagger` was split into `wait_for_stagger_slot` plus the spawn so the scope can hold its admission gate across `spawn` alone. The shared-timestamp semantics are unchanged: the slot is still claimed under the shared lock and released before the process is created.
- `ResolveCommandRepairAgent` and the conflict-resolve entry points now take the invocation's `AiCommandRunner` in place of the `SharedStaggerState` they used to build one from. This replaced a parameter rather than adding a cleanup-specific one, keeping operation APIs free of scope plumbing.
- The scoped runner treats a dropped `StreamingChildHandle` as cancellation. Unscoped runners (the TUI's standalone worktree command and in-crate helpers) keep their existing caller-owned behaviour, so nothing outside a scheduler run changed.
- `src/tui/mod.rs` re-exports `shutdown_local_orchestrator_task` so the heavy Unix regression drives the real local-quit path instead of a reimplementation of it. `src/lib.rs` exposes `ai_command_runner` for the same reason.

## Scope Notes

- `run_scope_run_fatal_cleans_process_group` models the queue boundary's prompt `Error` publication with a channel rather than driving `handle_merge_result_with_tx`, because the merge-result channel double that reaches that owner is `#[cfg(test)]` and therefore unreachable from `tests/`. The Error-before-barrier ordering against the real owner is pinned by the in-crate `run_fatal_error_precedes_cleanup_barrier`; the heavy test pins the process outcome at the failure-return boundary with a real SIGTERM-immune group.
- The construction regression combines a runtime scope-identity assertion (service → executor → shared runner) with a source-level guard that no run-owned construction file builds a runner outside `AiCommandRunner::for_run`. The eight operation surfaces reach their runner by cloning that one shared instance, so the guard is what keeps a new surface from reintroducing an unscoped runner.
